--- title: Research description: First-principles research on quantum materials, low-dimensional nanostructures, and electronic-structure and many-body methods. ---

Discovering and understanding real materials from first principles

We combine quantum theory with high-performance computing to understand how electrons behave in real materials. In quantum materials, interacting electrons and lattice vibrations can give rise to emergent phenomena such as superconductivity, exotic quasiparticles, and collective excitations. We aim to understand the quantum properties of materials from first principles and to explain and predict experimental observations. We also explore how atomic structure and dimensionality shape electronic properties, guiding the design of new nanostructures.

Electronic structure and many-body interactions in quantum materials

We use density functional theory and quantum many-body theory to study electronic structure, electron–phonon interactions, and electron–electron interactions in quantum materials. We investigate the microscopic mechanisms behind superconductivity and other ordered phases, and predict the properties and experimental signatures of quasiparticles and collective excitations.

  • Multivalley polarons in doped WS2 · Nature Communications (accepted)
  • Imaging and manipulating single electrons on a solid surface · Science (accepted)
  • Acoustic plasmons and pairing in Sr2RuO4 · Physical Review B (2024)

Atomic and electronic structure of low-dimensional nanostructures

We use first-principles calculations to study the stability and electronic properties of low-dimensional nanostructures. We examine how atomic geometry and bonding shape electronic behavior, and use this understanding to design new structures with tailored properties.

  • Phosphorus nanorings and nanohelices · ACS Nano (2025)
  • Magnetic MX3 chains · Advanced Materials (2023)

Development of ab initio electronic-structure and many-body methods

We develop first-principles methods based on density functional theory and quantum many-body theory. We also explore compressed representations of electronic states and interactions.

  • Phonons and excitons in twisted WSe2/MoSe2 · ACS Nano (2023)
  • Electron–phonon coupling in graphene moiré flat bands · Physical Review Letters (2021)
